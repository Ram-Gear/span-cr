Glossary of abbreviations

What it does

Given the gear blank data and the chordal addendum (CA) and min/max chordal thickness (CT) that you'd set on a gear tooth (B&S) caliper, Span CR converts them to the equivalent span measurement over N teeth (normal plane) and a comparator reading (CR), for stub tooth herringbone gears or pinions with basic addendum 0.8/DPT.

Inputs: NG/NP teeth, DPT (transverse DP), PAT (transverse PA), HA (helix angle), OD, CA, CT min, CT max, and the teeth to span over. The app also shows the theoretical number of teeth to span and the minimum face width needed to take the span on a helical tooth.

Ask Grok: how do chordal thickness and span relate?

Practice 1 – the spreadsheet example (span)

142 teeth, 2.5 DPT, 20° PAT, 30° HA, OD 57.12, CA 0.1608, CT 0.4262 / 0.4294. Span over 16 teeth?

Show answer

MAX 16.6779, MIN 16.6748. (BPN = 1.0379379; span = 15 × BPN + CTTN at BD, 1.10878 max / 1.10572 min.) Theoretical teeth to span = 16.38, so 16 is the natural choice.

Glossary

NG / NP – Number of teeth, gear / pinion
Tooth count of the part being measured. Enter whole teeth.
N – Number of teeth
Same as NG/NP; also used for the number of teeth a span is taken over.
DP / DPT – Diametral pitch / transverse diametral pitch
Teeth per inch of pitch diameter, measured in the plane of rotation (transverse). Herringbone gears are usually specified by DPT.
PA / PAT – Pressure angle / transverse pressure angle
Tooth flank angle at the pitch circle in the transverse plane, in degrees (e.g. 20).
PAN – Normal pressure angle
Pressure angle in the plane square to the tooth (normal plane), in degrees. The app works it out from PAT and HA; span is a normal-plane measurement.
HA – Helix angle
Angle of the teeth to the gear axis at the pitch diameter, in degrees (e.g. 30).
BHA – Base helix angle
Helix angle at the base circle, in degrees. Used for the minimum face width needed to take a span.
OD / ODG / ODP – Outside diameter (gear / pinion)
Actual measured outside diameter of the blank, in inches. The chordal addendum is measured from it.
PD – Pitch diameter
N ÷ DPT, in inches. For a single gear (or pinion) on standard centers the operating pitch diameter PDE equals PD.
BD – Base diameter
PD × cos PAT, in inches: the circle the involute is generated from.
BPT / BPN – Base pitch, transverse / normal
Distance between neighbouring tooth flanks along the base circle, in inches. BPN (normal) is the step added for each extra tooth in a span.
CA – Chordal addendum
The depth setting on a gear tooth (B&S) vernier caliper: distance from the OD down to the chord where thickness is measured, in inches.
CT – Chordal thickness
Tooth thickness read on the gear tooth caliper at the CA depth, in inches. Enter the minimum and maximum of the tolerance.
CTTN / CTTT – Circular tooth thickness, normal / transverse
Arc tooth thickness (not the straight chord) in inches. CTTT is measured in the transverse plane (plane of rotation); CTTN in the normal plane, square to the helix. The app shows them at D′, at PD and at BD. For a herringbone gear, CTTN = CTTT × cos of the helix angle at that diameter.
B&S – Brown & Sharpe gear tooth vernier caliper (“chordal dimensions”)
The caliper check defined by chordal addendum (CA) and chordal thickness (CT). The original program is named “B&S to Span & CR” because it converts these chordal dimensions for an individual gear or pinion to span dimensions and comparator readings.
D′ – Diameter at the B&S measuring point
Diameter where the caliper jaws touch the tooth, in inches.
h – Height correction
Small difference (inches) between the caliper's CA and the true radial depth to D′.
a – Tooth angle
Angle (degrees) subtended by the chordal thickness at D′. On the help page “a” also means PD ÷ N.
X – Addendum modification factor (the X in the Farrel formula)
In inches. The app solves the Farrel formula backwards: X = ((CTTT at PD ÷ (PD/N)) − π/2) ÷ (2 × tan PAT × DPT). Positive X means a thicker tooth than standard.
Span – Span measurement (base tangent length)
Distance over a set number of teeth measured with disc micrometers, in inches.
CR – Comparator reading
In inches. Herringbone gear drawings (especially oilfield gears) often give only CR, OD, PAT and HA, so CR is the tooth-size spec the shop works to. The app shows it as CR = (OD − PD)/2 − 0.8/DPT − X. MAX CR comes from the min CT, MIN CR from the max CT (in the original sheet).
DPB / AB / PAB / TB – Basic DP / basic addendum / basic pressure angle / basic tooth thickness
Basis the CRs are referenced to: DPB = DPT, AB = 0.8/DPT (stub tooth), PAB = PAN in degrees, TB @ AB as calculated by the original sheet, in inches.
MAX / MIN – Maximum / minimum
Results from the max and min limits of chordal thickness.
CPT / CPN – Circular pitch, transverse / normal
Tooth-plus-space arc at the pitch diameter, in inches. For a gearset at the operating pitch diameter: CPT = CTTT of pinion + CTTT of gear + BLCT (and the same in the normal plane with CTTN and BLCN). The app doesn't show these; they are here to explain the Farrel formula.
PDE – Operating (effective) pitch diameter
Pitch diameter at which a gear runs in mesh, in inches. For a single gear, as in this app, PDE = PD.
BL / BLCT / BLCN – Backlash / circular backlash, transverse / normal
Clearance between mating teeth, in inches. BLCN is taken as “feeler gage backlash”. The set backlash isn't always split equally between gear and pinion. Not an input to this app (it treats one gear on its own).
INV – Involute function
inv(φ) = tan φ − φ (φ in radians). INVPAT is the involute of the transverse pressure angle; INVPATE is the same at the operating pressure angle (equal to INVPAT for a single gear). Used inside the app's calculation of CTTT at BD and PD.

Farrel formula (reference)

CTTT @ PDE = PDE × ((π/2 + 2 × tan PAT × DPT × X) / N + INVPAT − INVPATE)

The formula gives CTTT at PDE only when PDE, OD, DPT, PAT, the number of teeth and the comparator reading are known. For an individual gear or pinion: PDE = PD, INVPAT = INVPATE, C = CE and RC = 1.000. That is the case this app uses, and it then reduces to CTTT @ PD = (PD/N) × (π/2 + 2 × tan PAT × DPT × X).

Source: R.C. Blake Company, Explanation of the Farrel Formula (Farrel manual #610A, pp. 45-46).

Notes on the original

This app reproduces the spreadsheet cell-for-cell, including its rounded constants (π = 3.141592654). One quirk is kept on purpose: the MAX-thickness "CTTT at BD" divides by the MIN-thickness D' (cell J54 uses I37). The effect is ~2×10⁻⁸ in., far below display precision.
