What it does

Given the gear blank data and the chordal addendum (CA) and min/max chordal thickness (CT) that you'd set on a gear tooth (B&S) caliper, Span CR converts them to the equivalent span measurement over N teeth (normal plane) and a comparator reading (CR), for stub tooth herringbone gears or pinions with basic addendum 0.8/DPT.

Inputs: NG/NP teeth, DPT (transverse DP), PAT (transverse PA), HA (helix angle), OD, CA, CT min, CT max, and the teeth to span over. The app also shows the theoretical number of teeth to span and the minimum face width needed to take the span on a helical tooth.

Practice 1 – the spreadsheet example (span)

142 teeth, 2.5 DPT, 20° PAT, 30° HA, OD 57.12, CA 0.1608, CT 0.4262 / 0.4294. Span over 16 teeth?

Show answer

MAX 16.6779, MIN 16.6748. (BPN = 1.0379379; span = 15 × BPN + CTTN at BD, 1.10878 max / 1.10572 min.) Theoretical teeth to span = 16.38, so 16 is the natural choice.

Practice 3 – other spans & face width

Same gear: spans over 17 and 15 teeth, and minimum face width for 16?

Show answer

17 teeth: 17.7158 / 17.7127. 15 teeth: 15.6399 / 15.6369. Min face width for 16 teeth: 7.953 (span × sin base helix angle 28.48°).

Notes on the original

This app reproduces the spreadsheet cell-for-cell, including its rounded constants (π = 3.141592654). One quirk is kept on purpose: the MAX-thickness "CTTT at BD" divides by the MIN-thickness D' (cell J54 uses I37). The effect is ~2×10⁻⁸ in., far below display precision.
